# Level: how much the imagery carries

A face and a palette applied alone give a flat page. The imagery and its finish give the character, and they are judged with the rest: the same quiet photo is right under a calm direction and a failure under a daring one.

## Read the level in the direction
Look at what is already decided, in this order:

1. **The brief.** A stated wish wins: "sober", "like a film poster", "tactile".
2. **The colour, type and layout entries** in `decisions.md`: their level and their gestures. The imagery takes the layout's level unless the brief says otherwise.
3. **The material at hand.** Strong photos, scans or objects raise the level; no material at all keeps it calm rather than filled with stand-ins.
4. **The trade**, last. It suggests, never dictates.

| Level | The imagery | The finish |
|---|---|---|
| **Restrained** | One strong image per screen or none, a calm treatment, generous room around it | Discreet: an elevation shadow, a faint grain, a soft fade |
| **Assertive** | A visible treatment (duotone, halftone, cut-outs, a hard crop), images that bleed or overlap | Visible: a texture you notice, a layered depth, a clear light |
| **Radical** | The image is the page: full bleed, extreme crop, 3D, a generative piece, type as the image | The gesture itself: the matter, the light or the depth is what people remember |

Write the level with its reason: `Level: radical, the layout's level`, or `Level: restrained, under the type's, because the client's photos are few`.

## One idea, four voices
The colour, the type, the layout and the imagery each may carry a gesture, and the four express **one idea**. A cinema programme as a projection: ink-black ground for the colour, condensed headlines for the type, a wide frame for the layout, and for the imagery one beam of light across real film stills (light as the finish). A lawyer's practice as paper and stone: calm colour, a book face, a strict grid, and photos of real matter, paper fibre and stone, in one cool treatment (matter as the finish).

If two gestures pull apart, keep the stronger and calm the other. Four unrelated gestures read as too much.

## Clichés
Generated pages repeat these because they are common, not because the project needs them. Each one is allowed only with a reason on the entry's `Kept on purpose` line, a reason that comes from the brief, the plan or the inspirations.

- Circles, blobs or blurred glows drawn to fill a space (also `source-1`).
- A gradient blob or mesh behind the hero, by default.
- Stock scenes: a handshake, a laptop on a desk, a smiling team, a city at dusk.
- The trade's symbol: scales of justice, a gavel, a tooth, a stethoscope, a lightbulb for ideas.
- Glass cards everywhere, on a gradient.
- A soft shadow on every card.
- Emoji, sparkles, or an icon in front of every heading; a grid of icons standing in for content.
- A 3D render or abstract shape that says nothing about the project.

Competitors serve one purpose: naming what is overused in the sector, in one line on the entry. They are never examples, even when one looks good. Examples come only from the brief and the inspirations.

## Broken and fixed

| Broken | Fixed |
|---|---|
| Two orange circles behind the headline of a cinema | One real still, cropped hard, with a beam of light across it |
| A lawyer's hero with scales of justice | A close photo of paper fibre or stone in one cool treatment, or the practice's name set as the image |
| Grain, glass and a gradient on the same card | One finish, matter: grain on the large surfaces, the cards plain |
| A bold palette over a centred stock photo | The photo cut out and set over the colour field, bleeding off the edge |
