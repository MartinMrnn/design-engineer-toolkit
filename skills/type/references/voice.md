# Voice: how much risk the type carries

Typography is judged with the rest of the page. The same face can be right on a calm site and a failure on a bold one.

## Read the level in the direction
Look at what is already decided, in this order:

1. **The brief.** A stated wish wins: "sober", "loud", "like a magazine cover".
2. **The colour.** Its strategy and gesture, in `decisions.md`. Large fields of an unexpected colour take a risk; a neutral interface does not.
3. **The components and images.** Hard shapes, large crops or strong textures raise the level.
4. **The trade**, last. It suggests, never dictates. A bold bakery is not a fault.

| What the direction does | Level |
|---|---|
| Neutral interface, rare accent, content first | **Restrained** |
| A clear personality, one strong choice of colour or image | **Assertive** |
| Colour, shapes or images that take a visible risk | **Radical** |

If the direction is not decided yet, choose the level with the user. Write it with its reason: `Level: radical, because the ground is a saturated orange`.

## The failure this prevents
A site with daring colours and components, then a headline in the usual neutral sans. The risk taken everywhere else suddenly looks like an accident. At the **radical** level, the headline must be able to work almost as a logo. At the **restrained** level, the reverse holds: a shouting face on a quiet page is the same mismatch.

## One gesture
By default, a restrained base and **one** memorable typographic decision. Kinds of gesture, to choose from the project and never as a menu to fill:

- **Scale:** a headline far larger than anything else on the page.
- **Width:** a very wide or very condensed cut.
- **Character:** a display face with a strong drawing, close to a logotype.
- **Contrast of voices:** one word or line in a second face, such as a serif inside a sans title.
- **A rare cut:** an unusual weight, an optical size pushed to its extreme, a variable axis used on purpose.
- **Texture of text:** monospace labels, tight capitals, a recurring typographic mark.

A gesture is one idea. A huge title in a wild face with a mixed-font word is three, and reads as too much.

**The colour's gesture and the type's gesture must say the same thing.** A bold direction can carry both, as long as they serve one idea. If they pull apart, keep the stronger one and calm the other.

## Broken and fixed

| Broken | Fixed |
|---|---|
| A saturated, daring palette with a neutral grotesque headline | A headline with enough character to hold that palette |
| A quiet reading site with a poster face on every title | A fine text face, with the gesture in the scale of one title |
| "Luxury" answered by a thin high-contrast serif | The form the project needs, described and searched for |
| A display face in the paragraphs | The display face on a few words, a sober face for the text |
| Three gestures on one page | One, stated in a sentence |

## Competitors
A quick look at 3 to 5 sites of the sector, for one purpose: naming what is overused. Write one line, such as `Overused in the sector: thin luxury serifs`, and stop. Take no face, pairing or idea from them, even when one looks good. Examples come only from the brief, `design/inspiration/` and its `board.md`.
