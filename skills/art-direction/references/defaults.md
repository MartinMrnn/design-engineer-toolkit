# Defaults to question

Generated interfaces repeat the same moves because they are common, not because the project needs them. Each item below is allowed only when the brief, the B2 plan or the moodboard gives it a reason you can state in one sentence. Otherwise, leave it out or find the form that belongs to this project. The critic reads this list too (`../art-direction/references/defaults.md` from the critique skill).

## Indicators and badges
- A coloured status dot or pill ("open now" in green, "closed" in red, "live", "new") added by habit. If the information matters to an intent (opening hours for a visit), give it the form and the place that suit the project, and not on the hero by default.
- A small eyebrow pill above every headline.
- Notification counts, "trending" or "popular" badges, and stars or ratings with no real reviews behind them.

## Decoration standing in for content
- The feature grid: an icon, a short title and two lines, in three or four identical cards.
- Statistics nobody gave you ("10k+ happy customers"), logo rows with no logos, stacks of avatars.
- Emoji used as icons, sparkles, a "scroll down" mouse icon.
- Section numbers, rules or kickers on every section when they carry no meaning for the visitor.

## Effects without a role
- Shadows: only to show that something sits above (a panel, a menu, a dragged element).
- Blur and transparency (glassmorphism): only to keep text readable over a moving or busy background.
- Gradients: only when they come from the direction (light, material, the brand), never as a default background.
- Grain, noise and textures: only when the direction asks for physical matter, and made credibly (from real scans or real print logic), not as a filter over everything.
- Rounded cards with a soft shadow, placed by default: shapes follow the direction's shape logic (`references/layout.md`).

## Motion by default
- Every element fading up on scroll. A reveal guides the eye to something; most content simply stays put.

When you keep one of these, say why in the file's Sources or rules, so the critic can judge the reason rather than the habit.
