# Level: how much the motion carries

Motion is judged with the rest of the page. The same quiet answer to a press is right under a calm direction and too little under a daring one; a staged opening is right under a radical one and a failure under a calm one. Whatever the level, the base stays the same: the interface answers at once and feels fluid. The level only decides what comes on top.

## Read the level in the direction
Look at what is already decided, in this order:

1. **The brief.** A stated wish wins: "sober", "alive", "like a film opening". Scroll-driven motion appears only when the brief asks for it.
2. **The colour, type, layout and imagery entries** in `decisions.md`: their level and their gestures. The motion takes the layout's level unless the brief says otherwise.
3. **The product.** A tool used every day moves less than a site seen once; frequency beats taste (`purpose-2`).
4. **The trade**, last. It suggests, never dictates.

| Level | What moves | Kinds of gesture |
|---|---|---|
| **Restrained** | Only what answers the visitor: a press, a hover, an opening, a closing, a state that changes. Nothing enters at load, nothing moves with the scroll | a state that finishes in place (a button that becomes its own confirmation); one control that morphs; the stillness itself |
| **Assertive** | One choreographed moment more, on the main path | a card that opens into its detail as one object; a first screen that enters once, in under a second; one thing with weight on a spring with a light bounce |
| **Radical** | The motion is the gesture people remember | a page transition that carries the idea within 300 ms (a shared element); a scroll scene when the brief asks; a living object (a Rive file) that answers the visitor; type that moves as the image |

These are kinds to choose from with the project in mind, never a menu to fill. Write the level with its reason: `Level: assertive, the layout's level`, or `Level: restrained, under the layout's, because the brief asks for calm`.

## One idea, five voices
The colour, the type, the layout, the imagery and the motion each may carry a gesture, and the five express **one idea**. A running club as a stopwatch: a high-contrast ground, condensed numerals, a grid of laps, photos of real races, and for the motion the race times ticking into place once when the results load, everything else answering at once. A botanical archive as a herbarium: calm colour, a book face, a strict column, scans of pressed specimens, and motion almost still: a specimen card opens into its page as one object, and nothing else moves.

If two gestures pull apart, keep the stronger and calm the other. Five unrelated gestures read as too much; the motion is usually the one to calm.

## Clichés
Generated pages repeat these because they are common, not because the project needs them. Each one is allowed only with a reason on the entry's `Kept on purpose` line, a reason that comes from the brief, the plan or the inspirations.

- Every block fading up as it scrolls into view. In the measure, 3 of the 9 brand sites whose window scrolled reveal more than 10 % of their blocks this way, up to 48 %; among products only Resend does, on 11 %.
- A smooth-scroll library taking over the page's scroll (7 of 14 brand sites, no product).
- Parallax on background images.
- A loader or intro that holds the page before its content.
- A cursor follower, or magnetic buttons everywhere.
- Every heading split into letters that animate in.
- A ticker of logos or words, by default.
- A lift with a shadow on every card on hover.
- Sections pinned so the vertical scroll moves sideways.

Competitors serve one purpose: naming what is overused in the sector, in one line on the entry. To fill it, look at three or four sites of the trade for a few minutes (in a project, the researcher's board may already name them); otherwise write `not looked`. They are never examples, even when one moves well. Examples come only from the brief and the inspirations.

## Broken and fixed

| Broken | Fixed |
|---|---|
| A bakery's page where every paragraph fades up on scroll | Content in place; the order button becomes its own confirmation |
| A festival hero with a spinning record and letters flying in | One opening: the poster image settles once, the title is already there |
| A bold direction with default 300 ms `ease` on everything | Restrained base on custom curves, plus one morph on the main path |
| Lenis, parallax and a pinned section on the same page | Native scroll; the one scroll scene the brief asked for, scrubbed |
