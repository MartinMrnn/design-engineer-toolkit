# v0.4 · A creative process that explores, converges and explains

> Architecture spec · status: **proposed for validation** (2026-10-29, revision 3) · replaces the colour-strategies approach (branch `colour-strategies`, not released)
> Scope of v0.4: lots 1 and 2, then the comparative test (lot 4). v0.4 is released only if the test passes (§13.6). Lot 3 follows only then.

## 1. Why

Two blind tests on colour taught the same thing from different angles.
- The studio picked visual elements one by one and justified them afterwards. A palette chosen because its colours "mean" the intent was then spread over the page without a reason for where each colour goes.
- The process itself splits the design into separable families (`colour`, `type`, `shape`, `imagery` in `charterlib/branches.py`, mixable at C2). It has no governing idea that ties them together, and no place for what makes an interface singular beyond its tokens (the treatment of content, a material, a way of showing data).
- The colour strategies of the `colour-strategies` branch fixed this partly, but they assigned a strategy to each direction before the direction existed. They won "fits the intent" on 1 brief of 3.

Goal: a process that **builds a visual language from an intent, explores several of them cheaply, judges them in concrete compositions, diagnoses at the right level what does not work, and records why what works works**, without turning aesthetic judgement into mechanical rules.

## 2. Principles

1. **The UX guides the art direction; the art direction may enrich the UX.** The UX decides what the visitor must understand, be able to do, and in what priority. The art direction decides how that is perceived. A change to the functional structure is proposed, judged and approved, never decided by one discipline for the other (§4).
2. **The idea comes before its parts.** A direction starts from a governing idea; colour, type, composition, the treatment of content and motion are its manifestations. A colour strategy is something an idea chooses, never a slot assigned in advance.
3. **Nothing visual is judged alone.** A colour is judged in a situation (its role, its size and proportion, its neighbours, the content around it, the width of the screen). So is a typeface, a texture or a component.
4. **Diagnosis isolates; creation relates.** Tests that change one variable at a time locate a problem (§7). They never generate the design, and a variant always answers a stated question.
5. **Three registers, never mixed** (§7.3): perceptual observations inform, accessibility requirements pass or fail, creative judgement is argued.
6. **Diversity serves relevance.** Ideas are explored widely, but an idea is kept for its relevance to the project, not to fill a quota.
7. **Quality and cost are both measured.** The new flow is adopted on the comparative test's result and its real cost (§11), not on its number of steps.
8. **Accessibility is a design constraint, not an art direction** (§7.6). The applicable criteria are known from the first exploration; the art direction finds a singular, coherent and accomplished expression within them, and never trades one requirement for the other.

## 3. The five levels of a visual decision

| Level | What it decides | Typical fault |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent** | What the visitor must feel, understand and do (A1, B1) | A good-looking page that says something else than the brief |
| **Governing idea** | One sentence that links the product's subject to its form, and its manifestations | Choices right one by one that say nothing together |
| **Palette** | The hues and their character (OKLCH lightness and chroma) | An association that does not hold, whatever the distribution |
| **Distribution** | Which colour dominates, on which surfaces and components, in what proportion, next to what | Two touching surfaces at the same lightness; a saturated colour over a whole page |
| **Context** | How the language adapts to dense text, photos, states, mobile | An opening that works and components that fall flat |

**Functional hierarchy and visual hierarchy are distinct.** The UX ranks what matters (a rank, not a position). The art direction chooses freely how to express that rank: size, weight, colour, space, motion, position. The critic checks that the visual result serves the functional ranks. Visual hierarchy is an outcome observed with the tests of §7, not a level of decision.

## 4. The boundary between UX and art direction

| Stable before the specimens (the UX decides) | Open to the art direction | Negotiable (proposed by the art direction, approved by the user) |
|---|---|---|
| Intents per page, in the visitor's words | Layout: grid, positions, alignments | The order of sections or story beats |
| The main action and the main path, step by step | How a rank is expressed: size, colour, space, motion | Merging or splitting sections |
| The information and functions each intent needs | The form of a content: list, table, chart, sentence, image | What is shown at once and what is revealed on demand |
| The rank of each element within a screen | Visual grouping, rhythm, surfaces | Adding or removing an element; a form that changes what the visitor can do |
| Functional needs: forms, states, choices that need a cue | Image treatment, the representation of data | The mobile navigation pattern |
| "Left out" | The mobile composition, within the mobile constraints | Rewriting a headline so that it serves the idea |
| Constraints: conventions kept, accessibility, main action reachable on mobile, nothing that depends on hover | The look of the navigation | Motion that changes understanding, not only mood |

- **The test for a doubtful case**: does the change alter what the visitor can learn or do, or the order in which they meet it? Yes: it is a structural proposal. No: the art direction decides.
- A visual form that inverts a functional rank in practice (a decoration becomes the focal point instead of the main action) is a defect, found by the blur test and reported by the critic, not a negotiation.
- **How a proposal travels**: the art director writes it in the specimen's idea card (section, current, proposed, intent served, what the visitor gains and loses) and shows it in the specimen; the critic judges its relevance (phase 2, §8); the user approves or refuses, recorded in `design/decisions.md`; the UX designer updates `06-structure.md` in a short amendment mission. Nobody writes in the other discipline's files.
- **When**: proposals are open during the specimens and the deepening. After the choice of a direction, the structure is stable again; a later proposal remains possible, and the lead says what it costs (pages already built).

## 5. The flow

| Step | Who | Produces | Stable at the end | The user |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 Kickoff | lead | brief, `Colour intent` line (the appetite is a **preference or a constraint**, never a strategy), content inventory | the brief | approves the brief |
| B1 Product | ux | research, `02` to `05` | the product | picks the key messages, approves |
| C1 Moodboard reading, **in parallel with B1** | art director | `07-visual-intent.md` (Keep, Avoid, Keywords, read as relations), 4 idea cards | what to keep and avoid | approves the reading; sees the cards only if the critic flags a diversity problem or the user asks |
| B2 Structure | ux, critic | the priority schema in `06-structure.md`, with the copy; optional wireframe or interaction cards | intents, path, ranks, needs, copy | approves |
| C2 · Specimens | 1 art director (resumed once), critic | 4 specimens, a local board | nothing visual: hypotheses | keeps 2, or 1 |
| C2 · Directions | 2 art directors in parallel (1 if the user kept one), critic | 2 branches, the published board | the language | chooses; the kept branch is completed |
| C3 to E1 | as in v0.3 | mockups, motion, build, review | frozen at C4 | as in v0.3 |

The nine step ids stay (A1 to E1); C2 holds two rounds. Dependencies:
- C1 needs only the brief, so it runs alongside B1. The idea cards need the intent and the key messages: the lead resumes the C1 art director with them after B1 (C1 stays one step, so this keeps the rule of one agent per step).
- The specimens need B2: they use its ranks and its real copy.
- C3 and later read the charter as today.
- Back loops are targeted (§7.2): a fault found at a level reopens that level only, within the existing stopping rules (two critic rounds per item, then the user decides).

## 6. Ideas, specimens and directions

### 6.1 Idea cards (C1)
- `design/ideas/<id>.md`, 4 by default. Each holds: the idea in one sentence; its manifestations (where it shows in colour and its distribution, type, composition, the treatment of content, motion if the idea moves); what it refuses; why it fits this project; the accessibility requirements it puts at risk and how it means to meet them (§7.6); the source it came from.
- **Sources are prompts for search, not quotas**: the product's subject, its real content, the visitor's world, the strongest relation in the moodboard, an existing identity. Several ideas may come from one source; an idea is kept for its relevance and its distinctness, never to cover a source. The card names its source only for traceability.
- The critic reviews the four cards for **distinctness** (text only, cheap): two cards that are variants of one idea are reported, whatever their sources.

### 6.2 Specimens (C2, first round)
- One art director pilots the four specimens, so the comparison stays homogeneous. It makes two, reports, and the lead resumes it for the other two (§6.4).
- `design/specimens/<id>/`: `index.html`, `tokens.json` (a partial palette and type are enough: `colour.py palette` measures a partial palette), `tokens.css`, `idea.md` (the card, completed with the distribution argued and any structural proposal), and captures at 1440 and 390 px, plus grey and blurred captures (§7).
- The art director measures its pairs (`palette`, `contrast`) while it builds each specimen, and resolves a failing applicable criterion as §7.6 says before the critique.
- **Three situations from B2**, with the real copy: the opening, one dense section, one interaction with its states. Concrete enough to judge composition, not only mood or palette.
- No Artifact, no documented components, no Cover. The local board (`skills/studio/references/board.html`) shows the four.
- The idea text never appears inside `index.html` (the blind critique depends on it, §8).

### 6.3 Directions (C2, second round)
- `charter.py branch a b` (the scripts already handle any number of branches). Two art directors in parallel, each from a kept specimen and its card.
- The `Showcase` card becomes the three situations, built on the branch's tokens and components (`Button`, `Card`), at 1440 and 390 px.
- Diagnostic variants are made by hand, on demand only (§7).
- The published board compares the two; the user chooses; the kept branch is completed as today.
- **No family mix by default.** Combining two ideas produces a new specimen. A `merge --take` stays possible, but it triggers a new rendering of the situations and a coherence review.

### 6.4 The art director's turn limit (verified)
- The art director has `maxTurns: 80`. A v0.3 direction took 73 to 82 turns, so four specimens in one run would not fit.
- Verified in the real trial transcripts: the limit applies **per run**, and a resumed agent keeps its context. In the coff'in trial, a critic stopped "at its 40-turn limit (partial result; SendMessage to task-id to continue)" and finished after a resume (42 turns in total). In the v0.3 trial, direction C ran 65 turns, then 17 after a resume (82 > 80).
- So: the specimen mission covers two specimens; the lead resumes the same agent for the other two.
- Fallback, if a later Claude Code release counts turns across resumes: raise the art director's `maxTurns` to 120 (the frontend developer already has 120). If one context then grows too large, split into two agents of two specimens, the second reading the first's cards and captures. Running specimens in parallel stays an option, adopted only if a trial shows it helps.

## 7. Diagnosis

### 7.1 The tests

| Test | What changes | What it shows | Level it points to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blind reading | Nothing; a reader without the brief describes the page in three words | The intent perceived against the intent sought | Intent or idea |
| Grey | The hue is removed (a capture filter) | Whether the ranks hold by lightness alone; touching colours at the same lightness | Distribution (lightness) |
| Blur | The detail is removed (a capture filter) | Masses: where the eye goes, which colour weighs too much | Distribution (proportion) |
| Permutation | Same palette, roles swapped | Whether another distribution of the same palette works better | Distribution, rather than palette |
| Transposition | Same lightness and chroma, other hues | Whether the association or its character is at fault | Palette |
| Change of situation | Same language, another situation (dense text, form, 390 px) | Whether the language holds beyond the opening | Context |
| Removal | One element is removed (an accent, a texture, a colour) | Whether it carries the idea or decorates | Idea (simplify) |

All are labelled **Design heuristic**: practitioners' ways of seeing (the grey "value" check and the squint test come from painting and graphic design), not proofs.

### 7.2 How they are used
- **Only to answer a question** raised by the critic, the user or the art director's self-check. Each variant states its question; two or three variants per question at most. The studio never generates a batch of variants to pick a winner. The art director interprets.
- **Top-down order.** A wrong intent is not fixed by tuning colours. But a lower fault can blur a higher reading: when the blind reading fails, the grey and blur tests run before the idea is condemned.
- **Where it leads**: intent misread → back to the idea (or to the product, through the UX); ranks blurred → distribution; no permutation works → palette; holds in one situation only → a context rule; a content-form fusion → a structural proposal (§4).

### 7.3 Three registers
| Register | Examples | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceptual observation** | OKLCH lightness and chroma, the OKLCH lightness difference ΔL between touching surfaces, the contrast ratio between two surfaces that no criterion covers, shares from `dominant --match`, grey and blur captures, CVD distances | Information, stated with its figure and its meaning. Never a verdict, never a threshold. It may question a claim ("the accent stands out" when its share is tiny) |
| **Accessibility requirement** | A success criterion of §7.4 that applies to the pair or the element, named with its number and level | Pass or fail, compared unrounded. A failing requirement is a defect |
| **Creative judgement** | Is the idea strong, is the whole coherent, does it fit the intent | Argued by the critic, decided by the user |

Every output of `colour.py`, `checks.js` and every critic report keeps the three apart, with the label of the register.

**ΔL and the contrast ratio are two different measures**, always shown in separate columns, each with its own meaning:
- **ΔL** is the difference in OKLCH lightness L, a perceptual scale from 0 to 1 (OKLab, CSS Color 4): it estimates how far apart two colours *look* in lightness. Clay `#f0d3bc`, L 0.88507, against sage `#d6e3cf`, L 0.90054: **ΔL = 0.01546**, from unrounded values. (Subtracting the rounded figures, 0.901 − 0.885, would give 0.016; outputs and tests use the unrounded value.)
- **The contrast ratio** is WCAG's ratio of relative luminances, (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05) (WCAG's L1 and L2 are the lighter and darker relative luminances, not OKLCH lightness), from 1 to 21, where relative luminance is linear light, not perceived lightness: the measure the accessibility criteria use. Clay has a relative luminance of 0.68745, sage 0.73739: **1.0677:1**, displayed 1.07:1.
- The two are related but not interchangeable: ΔL says how different the lightnesses look; the ratio says whether a requirement is met where one applies.
- **Which requirement applies depends on the pair, not on the colours**: text on a ground or a surface → 1.4.3; the visual information that identifies a component, its state or its focus, against its adjacent colours → 1.4.11; two painted section panels → **no criterion**, unless their boundary is needed to identify a component or a graphic. For clay against sage, as two panels, both figures are observations: they explain the round-2 lesson and inform the grey test; they set no aesthetic threshold and no accessibility verdict. `palette` prints them in two columns labelled information, with no pass or fail.

### 7.4 Accessibility: what is required, reported or a studio rule

Three kinds of rule, never mixed:
- **Normative and required**: the WCAG 2.2 success criteria of levels A and AA listed below. WCAG defines level AA conformance as satisfying "all the Level A and Level AA success criteria" (WCAG 2.2 Recommendation, §5.2.1 Conformance Level), so level A is part of the gate.
- **Normative but only reported**: level AAA criteria (1.4.6, 2.4.12, 2.4.13, 2.5.5). Never part of a pass or fail.
- **Studio rules**: choices of the charter, checked as conformity, never presented as WCAG: targets of at least 44 px (`target-min`); the focus ring drawn as an outline outside the component in `focus-ring`, re-pointed to `on-surface-n` on a surface; text never directly on a tone; every content visible with reduced motion.
- **Advisory material** (Understanding pages, techniques, axe best-practice rules) informs the critic; it is never a gate and is not run.

The requirement column paraphrases; the exact normative text is quoted in Appendix A, read on the W3C Understanding pages of WCAG 2.2.

|---|---|---|---|

### 7.5 Coverage: automated, by hand, not covered

**Automated** (a pass here is evidence on these rules only, never a proof of WCAG conformance):
- `charter.py check` and `colour.py palette`, on the declared token pairs: 1.4.3 for each text token on the grounds its usage note names and each `on-surface-n` on its surface; 1.4.11 for `focus-ring` against `ground` and `ground-raised`.
- `checks.js`, axe-core 4.14.0, on the rendered pages at 1440 and 390 px. **Measured on 2026-10-29**: with today's tags (`wcag2a`, `wcag2aa`, the WCAG 2.0 rules), `target-size` does not run at all and two 16 × 16 px buttons pass unflagged; `target-size` is disabled by default in axe (`enabled: false`, tags `wcag22aa`, `wcag258`). With `runOnly` on the tags `wcag2a`, `wcag2aa`, `wcag21a`, `wcag21aa`, `wcag22aa`, it runs and flags the 16 px buttons as a serious violation; the extra tags add `label-content-name-mismatch` (2.5.3, A), `autocomplete-valid` (1.3.5, AA), `avoid-inline-spacing` (1.4.12, AA), `css-orientation-lock` (1.3.4, AA, marked experimental by axe) and `target-size` (2.5.8, AA).
- Two more gaps in today's `checks.js`: it keeps only violations of impact "serious" or "critical", so an A or AA violation of lower impact is dropped; and it ignores axe's "incomplete" results (needs review), such as text over images.
- **Lot 1 fixes `checks.js`**: the five tags above, no AAA and no best-practice tag; `target-size` also enabled explicitly (`rules: { 'target-size': { enabled: true } }`), so the check does not depend on axe's defaults; every violation of these rules reported whatever its impact, with its rule, success criterion, level and impact; rules axe marks experimental, and every "incomplete" result, reported as **needs review** for the critic, not as failures.

**By hand** (the critic and the self-checks, with the keyboard and the rendered page; each item recorded pass or fail with its evidence): 1.4.1 (each state has a word, icon, shape or weight; `cvd` only points at pairs to look at); 1.4.3 for text over images or gradients and the "needs review" items; 1.4.11 beyond the tokens (field borders, controls, their states, graphics, the rendered focus against its adjacent colours); 2.1.1 and 2.1.2 on the agreed interactions; 2.4.3; 2.4.7; 2.4.11 (a sticky header over the focus).

**Not covered** by the studio's checks or by the comparative test, among others: 1.4.4 Resize Text, 1.4.10 Reflow at 320 CSS px (the 390 px overflow check is a design check, not this criterion), 1.4.12 Text Spacing beyond axe's inline-style rule, 1.4.13 Content on Hover or Focus, 2.2 timing, 2.3.1 flashes, 2.5.1 to 2.5.4 and 2.5.7 pointer and dragging, 3.2.6 Consistent Help, 3.3 forms (errors, labels beyond axe, redundant entry, accessible authentication), and any test with assistive technology. The deliverable is a static mockup: **the studio never claims WCAG conformance**, only that the criteria above were checked as stated.

### 7.6 Accessibility is a design constraint, not an art direction

Accessibility and visual quality are both requirements. Neither is traded for the other: a mandatory criterion is never waived for beauty, and a direction is never flattened into a generic one to pass a check.
- **Known from the start.** The art director explores with §7.4 in hand. Each idea card names the requirements its idea puts at risk (light text on a vivid flood, a thin display face for body copy, a state told by colour, a focus ring on a busy texture) and how the idea means to meet them. Specimens run `palette` and the contrast of their pairs while they are built, not after.
- **When a combination fails a criterion that applies**, the art director looks for another way to materialise the idea before settling on a fix: change the colour's role or place (the vivid colour as a surface with its `on-surface` ink rather than as text), its size (and the large-text threshold only where the text is truly large), its lightness while keeping its hue and character, or add a cue that belongs to the idea's language. It records the two or three alternatives it tried and why it kept one.
- **No silent generic substitution.** Replacing a colour, a typeface or a component by a neutral default only because it passes (grey instead of the brand colour, the browser's focus ring, a system font) is a design decision: it must be stated, and the critic judges what it costs the idea.
- **When the idea and a requirement cannot be reconciled**, the art director says so with the alternatives tried, and the user decides between ideas. The requirement stays; the idea is not dropped by default either.
- The **critique** keeps the two apart (§8): conformity to the criteria is a fact; whether the accessible result is coherent, singular and accomplished is a judgement. A conforming interface is not for that reason a well-designed one.

## 8. Critique

- **Two phases, with a lock.**
  - Phase 1 (blind): the mission gives only the preview links. The critic writes its blind reading into the report file first: the intent perceived in three words, the focal point, the grey and blur observations.
  - Phase 2 (with the card): the lead resumes the same critic with the idea cards. It adds its reading and cannot rewrite phase 1. The gap between what the page says and what the card claims is part of the evidence.
- **Two separate judgements** per specimen and per direction:
  - **The idea**: specific to the project (could a competitor use it unchanged?), generative (asked about a situation it has not met yet, such as the 404 page or a form error, does it say what to do?), carried by its elements (the removal test).
  - **The execution**: each problem with its level (§3) and the test that shows it.

  | | Execution good | Execution improvable |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Idea strong** | Deepen | Deepen, fixing the level at fault |
  | **Idea weak** | Do not deepen: a polished generic | Drop |
- **Criteria**: "Consistency" splits into **Coherence** (the whole serves one idea; from C2) and **Conformity** (the work follows the charter; from C3). The rule "do not reward effort or quantity" stays. The critic never sees the maker's report, as today; the idea card is a deliverable, read only in phase 2.
- The diversity review of the idea cards (§6.1) is a short critic mission on text.
- **Accessibility facts and creative judgement are reported apart.** The report has its own block, criterion by criterion (§7.4 and §7.5): pass, fail or needs review, with evidence and the way it was checked (automated or by hand). That block is not a score: a failing required criterion rejects the work on its own (replacing today's rule that makes it the anchor of craft 1), and a "needs review" item stays open until it is checked. The scores judge the design: a fix that made the result generic, or an accessibility conflict solved by a silent substitution (§7.6), lowers Coherence or Distinctiveness; a conforming page is not for that reason a good one. The critic checks the requirements each idea card names as at risk.

## 10. Colour

- The six strategies of `composition.md` become a vocabulary of distribution that an idea may choose, still labelled Design heuristic. The method's step 3 moves inside the specimen: the idea decides the distribution, the strategy names it if one fits.
- The appetite of A1 is recorded as a preference or a constraint, and narrows nothing by itself.
- Round 2's three lessons enter the skill as **Design heuristics**, with their origin and their measures, never as thresholds:
  - touching surfaces read as one when they differ in hue but hardly in lightness (clay `#f0d3bc` against sage `#d6e3cf`: ΔL 0.01546 in OKLCH lightness; WCAG contrast ratio 1.0677:1; two observations, no criterion applying to two panels, §7.3);
  - the larger a surface, the more its chroma weighs (a pink `#FF61A6`, OKLCH C 0.2019, over a whole page read as heavy);
  - an identity colour may stay a signature instead of becoming the action colour.
- `colour.py palette` adds, as information, **ΔL in its own column** next to the contrast ratio it already gives for neighbouring tones and for each surface against the ground, and adds each pair of surfaces. Neither column carries a pass or fail. Its tests assert the two figures separately, unrounded, on the clay and sage pair (ΔL 0.01546, ratio 1.0677).
- The area effect (a colour tends to look lighter and more saturated on a large area than on a swatch) is cited as an empirical finding only after its source is checked; until then it is not written in the skill.
- `emotion` stays out until the Ou et al. 2004 papers are read (unchanged from the colour expertise spec).

## 11. The comparative test (lot 4)

Decided with the user before any run; nothing here changes once the first run starts.

### 11.1 Question and baseline
- **Question**: does the v0.4 exploration produce directions that fit the intent better and are more coherent, at an acceptable cost?
- **Baseline**: **v0.3** (branch `v0.3`, 53745e5; it won round 2), not `colour-strategies`.

### 11.2 The same information for both flows
- **One brief pack per brief, prepared once and frozen before any run**, approved by the user: the brief, the charter's `01` to `05` (one B1 run, shared), one moodboard and one image set, and **one structure text**: per page and per section, its role, its intent, the rank of its elements, its needs, its copy, the main path and the mobile constraints (the v0.4 format of `06-structure.md`).
- **v0.4** receives the structure text as its `06-structure.md`.
- **v0.3** receives **the same text** as its `06-structure.md` (ranks included: the information is identical), plus the wireframe cards its C2 needs. They are drawn by v0.3's own B2 UX agent from the pack, with one constraint: the same sections, the same order and the same copy, word for word. The layout those wireframes impose is part of v0.3's method, which is what the test compares.
- Same model and effort, fresh sessions, the plugin of each version installed from its own branch. **Fonts are not fixed** (round 2 froze type and photos, so colour was judged out of its context). Neither flow publishes Artifacts during the test (symmetrical; costs are compared relatively).
- **In-flow choices** (in v0.4, two specimens then one direction; in v0.3, one direction at C2) go through "choose for me", the independent comparison, in both flows.
- **What is judged**: the chosen direction rendered in the same three situations (opening, dense section, interaction) at 1440 and 390 px, by the same render mission in both flows.

### 11.3 Four measures, kept apart
1. **Blind judgement** by the user: shuffled labels, a key the user does not see. For each brief, a **forced choice** between X and Y on each question, as in rounds 1 and 2, so a brief never ends in a tie; the user may add "clear" or "slight" and a comment, which are reported and never counted. Questions: **fits the intent** and **coherence** (decisive); "I would build on it" and "the composition puts colour to work" (reported).
2. **Accessibility**, on the v0.4 renders, absolute: zero failure on the required A and AA criteria listed in the §7.4 table (not every WCAG A and AA criterion), as §7.5 checks them (automated: `check`, `palette`, `checks.js` with its five tags; by hand: the list of §7.5, each item recorded with its evidence). A "needs review" item counts as a failure until the critic records it as passing. This is stricter than "no regression": v0.3's results are recorded for information and do not move the gate. AAA is reported, never counted; the criteria §7.5 leaves uncovered are not tested and the result claims nothing about them. How each accessibility conflict was resolved (§7.6: alternative found, or generic substitution) is reported as information.
3. **Conformity** to the contract, on the v0.4 renders, absolute: `charter.py check` and `freeze` pass, and the critic, reviewing the renders as at C3, gives Conformity at least 3. v0.3's are recorded for information.
4. **Cost**: the weighted equivalent of the v0.3 retro (new input, cache write ×1.25, cache read ×0.1, output ×5), over the lead and every agent, **from C1 to the rendered situations**. The v0.3 wireframes drawn from the pack are reported separately and left out of the ratio, which is conservative for v0.4 (in real use it saves them). Overhead = Σ cost v0.4 ÷ Σ cost v0.3 − 1, over the briefs played. Duration and the number of user interventions are reported.

The critic's levels are compared with the user's comments, as information on the diagnosis method.

### 11.4 Sequence and stopping
- Every result belongs to one version: a pair compared, a cost summed, a tally counted always pairs one v0.4 version with v0.3 on the same brief pack, never results from two v0.4 versions or two protocols.
- Relais (discreet appetite, identity kept, data content) and Tako (dominant appetite, photos) first; Halte only if needed. Each brief is judged once both of its flows have run.
- The session reads the key to apply the rule and tells the user only "the test continues" or "the test is decided", so the next judgement stays blind.
- After Relais and Tako, for each decisive question (intent, coherence), v0.4 stands at 2–0, 1–1 or 0–2:

| After two briefs | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Intent 2–0 **and** coherence 2–0 | Quality rule met; Halte is not played |
| Intent 0–2 **or** coherence 0–2 | Quality rule cannot be met; the test stops |
| Any other case (a 1–1 on either) | Halte is played; each question is decided by the majority of three |

### 11.5 Decision rule (all four must hold; nothing else enters the decision)
1. v0.4 wins **fits the intent** on the majority of the briefs played **and** wins **coherence** on the majority of the briefs played. A split (one won, the other lost) fails the rule: the redesign exists to make directions more coherent while fitting the intent, so both are required.
2. Accessibility (§11.3, 2) holds **on the criteria the protocol evaluates, and only on them**: the required A and AA criteria of the §7.4 table, checked as §7.5 says (automatically or by hand), plus the axe rules `checks.js` runs. Passing this gate validates nothing about the criteria §7.5 lists as not covered, nor any other WCAG criterion; the test's results state this in so many words, and never call the renders WCAG conformant.
3. Conformity (§11.3, 3) holds.
4. The overhead is **at most +50 %**, computed unrounded (exactly 50 % passes).

- **Ties**: none per brief (forced choice). Across briefs, a 1–1 after two briefs is settled by Halte (§11.4); with three briefs a majority always exists.
- **Contradictions**: winning one decisive question and losing the other is a failure, at the level of the totals; a brief that splits between the two questions simply counts in each tally.
- **Budget**: if the test reaches 76 M before it is decided, its outcome is "not decided", which counts as not passed for the release; it goes on only if the user raises the budget explicitly, before any further run.
- Any outcome other than a pass is a failure, handled as §13.7 says.

### 11.6 Budget and limits
- About 18 to 25 M equivalent per brief for both flows: 36 to 51 M when two briefs decide, 54 to 76 M with the third (cap: §11.5).
- **The v0.3 reference is frozen** once its runs end: its renders, captures, measures, cost and transcripts are archived unchanged and reused as they are by any new trial (§13.7).
- **Limits, stated in the results**: one judge; one run per flow and brief, so the flow's effect is mixed with run-to-run variation; the user has seen these briefs before. A practical check, not a proof.
- The harness and the results live with the other test material (outside the repository); this spec records the decision.

### 13.7 If the test fails
1. **Analyse**, in a short written report: which part of §11.5 failed; per brief, the user's comments, the critic's levels and the gap between the blind reading and the idea card; the cost per step and per agent. Classify each cause:
   - the ideas (weak or generic specimens);
   - the execution (directions, renders, prompts);
   - the cost (turns, context, number of agents);
   - the protocol (unequal inputs, a broken harness).
2. **Correct only what the causes point to**, as an amendment to this spec that the user approves before any new run. The decision rule (§11.5), the 50 % ceiling and the brief packs do not change.
3. **One new trial at most**, with the amended version, called v0.4.1:
   - v0.4.1 runs on **every brief of the sequence again**, from Relais and Tako, with the same stopping rule (§11.4); the first trial's v0.4 results are kept for the analysis and never mixed into the new tallies or costs;
   - it is compared with the **frozen v0.3 results** (§11.6), unchanged, under new blind labels; if the sequence reaches Halte and v0.3 never ran it, v0.3 runs Halte once, from the same pack, and that result is frozen in turn;
   - if the user recognises a v0.3 result, it is recorded as a limit of the trial; v0.3 is not run again;
   - the only exception is a protocol cause: the first trial is then void for both flows, both are run again under the corrected protocol, and no result of the void trial is reused.
4. **Then the user decides**: release v0.4, abandon it and keep v0.3 as the next release (v0.3 is not released yet: `main` holds v0.2.0), or add to v0.3 only the parts that do not depend on the flow (the accessibility precision, the colour measures).

## 15. Out of scope, risks, open points

- **Out of scope**: dark themes; gradients and textures as tokens (a texture stays a treatment the idea calls for, in `11-imagery-and-icons.md`); a colour or materials agent; competitor colour analysis by default; APCA (licence).
- **Risks**: four specimens may still be judged on mood if their situations are thin, so the critic checks that each holds the three situations; the art director's context may grow too large over four specimens (measure captures by script, split if needed, §6.4); one judge and one run per condition in the test; hand-checked criteria (1.4.1, 1.4.11 beyond the tokens, the keyboard and focus criteria) may miss a fault, so the critic records each with its evidence; the full `checks.js` will report more issues than today's, which the E1 gate already handles (fixed or accepted by the user).
- **Open, after the test**: whether to deepen one or two specimens by default; whether the variant script is worth building; whether the critic's level diagnosis matched the user's reading closely enough to rely on.

## Appendix A · WCAG 2.2 text used by this spec

### A.1 Normative source: the WCAG 2.2 Recommendation

**Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2**, W3C Recommendation, 12 December 2024: https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/ (latest version: https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/). Every quote below was checked word for word against this dated Recommendation on 2026-10-29; each item gives its section's fragment id.

- **Conformance level AA** (5.2.1, `#cc1`): "For Level AA conformance, the web page satisfies all the Level A and Level AA success criteria, or a Level AA conforming alternate version is provided."
- **1.4.1 Use of Color (A)** (`#use-of-color`): "Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element."
- **1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) (AA)** (`#contrast-minimum`): "The visual presentation of text and images of text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, except for the following:" Large Text: "Large-scale text and images of large-scale text have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1;" Incidental: "Text or images of text that are part of an inactive user interface component, that are pure decoration, that are not visible to anyone, or that are part of a picture that contains significant other visual content, have no contrast requirement." Logotypes: "Text that is part of a logo or brand name has no contrast requirement."
- **Large scale** (glossary, `#dfn-large-scale`): "with at least 18 point or 14 point bold or font size that would yield equivalent size for Chinese, Japanese and Korean (CJK) fonts"
- **1.4.6 Contrast (Enhanced) (AAA)** (`#contrast-enhanced`): "The visual presentation of text and images of text has a contrast ratio of at least 7:1, except for the following:" Large Text: "Large-scale text and images of large-scale text have a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1;" (incidental and logotype exceptions as in 1.4.3).
- **1.4.11 Non-text Contrast (AA)** (`#non-text-contrast`): "The visual presentation of the following have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against adjacent color(s):" User Interface Components: "Visual information required to identify user interface components and states, except for inactive components or where the appearance of the component is determined by the user agent and not modified by the author;" Graphical Objects: "Parts of graphics required to understand the content, except when a particular presentation of graphics is essential to the information being conveyed."
- **2.1.1 Keyboard (A)** (`#keyboard`): "All functionality of the content is operable through a keyboard interface without requiring specific timings for individual keystrokes, except where the underlying function requires input that depends on the path of the user's movement and not just the endpoints."
- **2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap (A)** (`#no-keyboard-trap`): "If keyboard focus can be moved to a component of the page using a keyboard interface, then focus can be moved away from that component using only a keyboard interface, and, if it requires more than unmodified arrow or tab keys or other standard exit methods, the user is advised of the method for moving focus away."
- **2.4.3 Focus Order (A)** (`#focus-order`): "If a web page can be navigated sequentially and the navigation sequences affect meaning or operation, focusable components receive focus in an order that preserves meaning and operability."
- **2.4.7 Focus Visible (AA)** (`#focus-visible`): "Any keyboard operable user interface has a mode of operation where the keyboard focus indicator is visible."
- **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) (AA)** (`#focus-not-obscured-minimum`): "When a user interface component receives keyboard focus, the component is not entirely hidden due to author-created content."
- **2.4.12 Focus Not Obscured (Enhanced) (AAA)** (`#focus-not-obscured-enhanced`): "When a user interface component receives keyboard focus, no part of the component is hidden by author-created content."
- **2.4.13 Focus Appearance (AAA)** (`#focus-appearance`): "When the keyboard focus indicator is visible, an area of the focus indicator meets all the following:" "is at least as large as the area of a 2 CSS pixel thick perimeter of the unfocused component or sub-component, and" "has a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 between the same pixels in the focused and unfocused states." Exceptions: "The focus indicator is determined by the user agent and cannot be adjusted by the author, or" "The focus indicator and the indicator's background color are not modified by the author."
- **2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced) (AAA)** (`#target-size-enhanced`): "The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels except when:" (equivalent, inline, user agent control, essential).
- **2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) (AA)** (`#target-size-minimum`): "The size of the target for pointer inputs is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, except when:" Spacing: "Undersized targets (those less than 24 by 24 CSS pixels) are positioned so that if a 24 CSS pixel diameter circle is centered on the bounding box of each, the circles do not intersect another target or the circle for another undersized target;" (and the equivalent, inline, user agent control and essential exceptions).

### A.2 Explanatory references: *Understanding WCAG 2.2* (informative)

*Understanding WCAG 2.2*, W3C: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/. Each page states that it holds "Informative explanations, not required to meet WCAG", and the Recommendation (5.1) defines notes and supporting material as informative. These pages reproduce the criteria; the spec takes their text from A.1 and uses the pages below only to explain how a criterion is applied.

- **Rounding** (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html): "the computed values should not be rounded (e.g., 4.499:1 would not meet the 4.5:1 threshold)." The same rule, for 3:1 (2.999:1), is stated in https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html.
- **Large scale in CSS pixels** (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html): 14 pt and 18 pt are "approximately 18.5px and 24px" (the spec's 18.67 px comes from 1 pt = 4/3 px, the value `colour.py` uses).
- **Focus indicators and 1.4.11** (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html): "Focus indicators are visual information required to identify a state of a user interface component. That means that they are subject to 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, in addition to 2.4.13 Focus Appearance." How the adjacent colours are chosen for an indicator outside, inside or on the border of a component: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html.
- **What "Level AA" includes** (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/conformance.html): explains Conformance Requirement 1, quoted from the Recommendation in A.1.
